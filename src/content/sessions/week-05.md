Weeks 3 and 4 were about how a wait is represented. This week the
representation starts doing work of its own.

Buell and Norton ran five experiments on simulated travel and dating services
and found people preferring the slower option when it showed its effort, with
the results identical. Perceived effort produced reciprocity, and reciprocity
produced value. Read the paper for the boundary conditions, which is where
the interesting arguments are.

The second case this week is student sourced and you bring it. An interface
that names each supplier as it queries them is the canonical shape, and once
you have seen one you will find the others quickly.

## In the workshop

Each of you brings one interface that performs effort. We sort them into two
piles: the ones where the effort shown is effort actually being spent, and
the ones where it is not. The second pile is larger than people expect, and
the argument about which pile a given case belongs in is the session.

## But wait, there's more

Go and watch a flight search that lists carriers as it checks them. The list
scrolls whether or not the queries run one at a time, and it keeps scrolling
after the results are in. Time how long the feed runs against how long the
page takes to become useful.
