You start by proving to yourself that the gap is real. Not by reading that it
is real, which takes a minute, but by measuring it on yourself, which takes a
week and is harder to argue with afterwards.

Maister sets up the vocabulary and Block and Zakay show you how carefully the
psychology has to be done. Read Maister for the propositions and Block and
Zakay for how much of the field's confidence survives a meta-analysis. They
disagree about how solid the ground is. That disagreement is useful to you.

## In the workshop

You build the instrument you will use for the Wait Log. Two columns and a
scale: clock duration, felt duration, and what you were told while it
happened. We test it on the room, badly, and then fix it. Bring a phone with
a stopwatch.

The point of building it yourself is that you find out what the third column
is for. Most people leave it blank the first time and then cannot explain
their own numbers.

## But wait, there's more

Look at a pedestrian crossing with a countdown timer next to one without. The
countdown does not change how long the lights take. It replaces an unknown
duration with a known one, and that is the entire intervention. Stand at each
for a few cycles this week and notice which one you check your phone at.
