Sterne treats the music in the Mall of America as architecture rather than
atmosphere: programmed, purchased, and doing a job the building needs done.
Read it for the method and not for the mall. The same reading applied to a
phone queue is this week.

The corpus is ours to build. In the workshop we assemble a set of hold
scripts from the cohort, transcribed rather than described, and read them as
written texts. A transcript makes visible what a summary hides, which is the
ordering and the timing.

## In the workshop

The workshop also covers the withholding technique this course is named
after. An infomercial interrupts its own close to add a second offer, and the
interruption is the mechanism: a thing about to end is made to continue, and
the continuation is presented as a gift rather than as an extension. The same
move runs in hold scripts and in checkout flows. Handle it as a technique
with a structure, because that is what it is.

Bring a transcript. Thirty seconds of it is enough.

## But wait, there's more

Time the loop on a hold system and it is often somewhere near fifteen seconds
before the recording repeats. Long enough that you do not notice the seam
straight away, short enough that the licence stays cheap. Listen for the
fifteen second loop the next time you are on hold and see how long it takes
you to hear the join.
