## The brief

> Present the Intervention and defend the choice of who it manages.

The defence is worth more than the presentation. You get twelve minutes, and
the room has one question it is instructed to keep asking, which is who your
intervention acts on and whether you are comfortable with that.

The defence is not against a rubric. It is against the three clauses of the
thesis on the front page of this course. Be ready to say which clause your
work leans on hardest, and which one it would refute if the work turned out
not to function.

## What you submit

Nothing in advance. The presentation is the submission and it happens in the
week 12 studio. Bring whatever you need on screen.

## How it is marked

Two criteria, and the second is the larger. A clear presentation of a
defensible intervention earns you most of the first. The second is about
whether you can be specific under pressure about the person your design
manages, without retreating to the user.
